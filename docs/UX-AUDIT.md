# Game UX audit: Untitled Tank Game

**November 19, 2026.** Reviewed the game at [the preview/launcher release](https://github.com/starharbor2491/utg/commit/63db84d) and the local flight-tuning update. The flight change is implemented; the recommendations below are not.

**Start with three things:** teach the controls of the vehicle the player actually picked, be explicit about keyboard/mouse requirements before Play on touch devices, and give both duelists a ready state. These remove observed failures. A new visual theme would not.

## What was tested

| Journey | Evidence and result |
|---|---|
| First launch and loadout | Opened Free Play, selected Apache, inspected its briefing and controls, deployed via Play. The model and selected row agreed; the expanded controls still described a tank. [Desktop briefing](ux-evidence/briefing-desktop.jpg) |
| Taking control | Flew both helicopters with mouse and keyboard, held throttle, flipped, rolled without yaw, tested Alt free-look and the optic. Checked the guide and its `H` shortcut, plus carrier, gun-drone and FPV behavior through gameplay regressions. [Flight HUD](ux-evidence/flight-desktop.jpg) |
| Reading a fight and recovering | Triggered a penetration through the existing QA hit probe and a KO through the QA kill hook. The hit review identified damaged parts; the respawn message gave a cause and countdown. Pausing Free Play for 300 ms left its match state unchanged. [Hit review](ux-evidence/damage-desktop.jpg) |
| Changing vehicle or mode | Paused away from base, selected Apache, resumed. The actual vehicle stayed an MBT as intended, but there was no specific “Apache queued” confirmation. The Free Play pause menu had no route to 1v1. |
| Multiplayer | Two browsers hosted/joined through public signaling. Tried malformed and nonexistent codes, changed the guest’s loadout, moved the host while the guest was still in the briefing, flew the guest Apache, and closed the guest. The host reported connection loss and offered “Return to start.” |
| Small screens and alternate input | Inspected 1280×720 desktop, 390×740 emulated touch, and a 640×360 compact menu. Tapped through deployment, inspected play in portrait, tested Tab navigation, focus on pause and keyboard preview controls. [Portrait lobby](ux-evidence/lobby-portrait.jpg) · [Portrait combat](ux-evidence/combat-portrait.jpg) |
| Accessibility and startup | Ran axe-core 4.10.3 on briefing, lobby and combat DOMs with WCAG A/AA tags; inspected layout, colors, input handlers, radar and camera code separately. Tested the rebuilt single HTML offline in three fresh Chromium processes. |

This is an expert review with repeatable functional checks, **not** a player study or accessibility certification. The screenshots and dimensions come from Chromium; touch was emulated, not a physical phone. No assistive-technology user sessions, Safari/Firefox comparison, low-end-device study or cross-network latency benchmark were run. Observations below are separated from proposed tests.

## Findings, in priority order

**P1** blocks a supported task, excludes a player, or materially affects fairness. **P2** leaves a workable path but causes recurring confusion or friction. **P3** is a smaller improvement worth validating.

| Priority | What happens and why it matters | Recommended change and acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 · Flight onboarding** | With Apache selected, the expanded **Quick controls** still says “Drive,” “Fire main gun,” and “Switch shell.” Shift/Ctrl for throttle only appears in separate vehicle copy and the live guide. A player consulting the detailed instructions can remain on the ground. | Reuse one per-vehicle control map in the briefing and HUD. For a first helicopter flight, teach just three things in order: raise throttle, pitch, then recover. Keep the rest available on demand. **Check:** a new player can take off and level out without leaving the game for instructions. [Hick](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/) · [GAG onboarding](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/) |
| **P1 · Touch deployment promises more than it delivers** | A touch user can inspect a model, choose a mode and tap Play, then receives a keyboard guide. There are no equivalent on-screen driving, aiming and throttle controls. At 390×740 the radar covered roughly **144×194 CSS px** of the vehicle panel, obscuring the right-hand status values. | Choose the supported experience explicitly. If desktop is the target, tell coarse-pointer users **before Play** that driving requires a keyboard/mouse; leave preview available. If touch play is intended, supply the inputs and a dedicated compact HUD. **Check:** a touch-only visitor knows what is playable before deploying; on supported sizes the radar never covers weapon or damage status. [XAG 107](https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xag-deep-dives/xag-107-input) |
| **P1 · Duel starts before both players are ready** | A guest was still looking at the loadout screen when the deployed host could already drive. Opening the online menu correctly does not freeze the opponent, but that rule also applies before the first deployment. Joining is not the same as consenting to start. | Separate “connected,” “ready” and “in battle.” Show both ready states, begin with a short shared countdown, then keep the current non-pausing behavior. **Check:** neither player can move or fire before both are ready; either player can back out of the lobby. |
| **P1 · Zoom and HUD readability** | `user-scalable=no` was the single axe violation in each of three states. Both the web entry and single-file packer set it. The control guide uses **11 CSS px**, radar range labels **8 px** on canvas. There is no player-facing HUD scale. Good contrast on the menu does not solve small lettering in motion. | Remove the zoom restriction from both entry points; use local gesture handling where the game needs it. Offer a HUD scale with reflow and a compact alternative. **Check:** browser zoom and large HUD text preserve Play/Resume, radar and critical status without clipping. Measure rendered glyph height on the target device, not just the CSS declaration. [XAG 101](https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xag-deep-dives/xag-101-text-display) |
| **P2 · Faster flight needs player-controlled sensitivity** | The new rotation rates improve agility, but mouse response, axis direction and bindings are fixed. The optic already scales sensitivity with FOV. `R` plus mouse is an alternative to A/D roll; it should not become the only way to roll. Camera shake remains active under reduced-motion emulation; the CSS preference only disables an overheat blink. | Keep the new default. Add aircraft sensitivity, invert-pitch and a shake-intensity setting down to zero; keep manual flips intact. Longer term, remap actions and generate hints from the bindings. **Check:** precision and high-sensitivity players can complete the same hover, turn and recovery task; disabling shake does not remove hit information. [XAG 107](https://devdocs.xbox.com/build/game-principles/accessibility/xag-deep-dives/xag-107-input) · [XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) |
| **P2 · Aircraft instruments borrow tank vocabulary** | Apache’s main readout combines two bare numbers (`0 / 0 M` at rest) with a small speed/altitude/throttle legend. Its gun heat is labeled “Coax,” while the loadout calls the weapon “Gatling.” The guide reports the bindings but does not tell a pilot whether they are climbing or inverted. | Group **speed**, **altitude/climb** and **throttle** with their own values and units. Use the selected weapon name. Test a small horizon/bank cue rather than adding another full panel. **Check:** pilots can distinguish stalled climb from low throttle and identify the weapon that is cooling at a glance. [Proximity](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/) |
| **P2 · Loadout confirmation and mode exit** | Away from base, the preview switched to Apache while the actual MBT remained active. The footer states a general rule, not which vehicle is queued. After entering Free Play, the mode picker is hidden; reloading is the discoverable workaround for starting a duel. | Show “Active: MBT · Next spawn: Apache,” with cancel/change, and add “Return to start” distinct from Resume. Confirm only when leaving would discard a match. **Check:** players can identify their next vehicle and start another mode without a browser refresh. [Jakob](https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/) |
| **P2 · Radar depends too heavily on color** | Inside radar range, both allies and enemies are filled diamonds. Their size, hue and heading ticks differ, but the shape is the same. The scoreboard uses absolute Blue/Red while the radar uses player-relative friend/foe colors; a red-team guest must translate between conventions. Off-range hostiles already use a chevron. | Keep the scoreboard team colors, add a “You” indicator, and use a redundant distinct shape for in-range hostiles. **Check:** an unfamiliar player on either team can identify friend, foe and self from a grayscale capture. [Similarity](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-similarity/) · [Hodent, game UX at Epic](https://celiahodent.com/ux-practices-epic-games/) |
| **P2 · Input capture and focus recovery** | The pointer-lock-denied path still supported flight input, yet the banner asked the player to open another tab. On portrait it overlapped the control guide. Tabbing past the preview also reached `BODY` before returning to the first button, despite the briefing declaring itself modal. Pause **did** focus Resume. | Distinguish “aim is working without lock” from “input unavailable.” Make the fallback banner dismissible, and keep modal focus in its controls with a clear way out. **Check:** refuse pointer lock, operate the menu with Tab/Shift+Tab and resume without opening another tab. [XAG 112](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112) |
| **P2 · Hit feedback competes with learning and aiming** | A penetration produced a center message and a detailed upper-right X-ray; the controls disappeared while the replay was active. The replay is valuable because it names the affected modules. It is less useful when someone only needs to know whether another shot is needed, or is still learning to fly. | Offer compact and detailed hit feedback. Preserve the short center result and weapon/part names; let players retain controls or choose the X-ray. **Check:** with detailed replay off, a player can still distinguish a miss, external damage, penetration and a kill. [Working memory](https://lawsofux.com/working-memory/) |
| **P3 · Next action in the lobby and on small screens** | Malformed input yielded “Enter the six-character room code.” A well-formed missing code yielded a useful retry message. The host’s code is selectable, but there is no Copy action. Disconnect ends with a reason and “Return to start,” not a rematch. On portrait, Free Play’s Play button started at **y=755** in a **740 px** viewport. It was scrollable, not missing. | Add Copy with confirmation and a manual fallback. Keep the primary action visible in the scrollable briefing, and retain the last loadout when starting a new room. **Check:** host/join a second match without retyping a shared code or hunting for Play. [Fitts](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/) · [Peak-end](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/) |

## Keep what works

- **Free Play works offline and really pauses.** Its single-button entry and predictable Resume focus are worth preserving. The standalone file was ready in **1.21, 1.22 and 1.34 seconds** in three fresh headless Chromium processes on this machine, with **zero HTTP requests**. This measures local startup, not first-load web performance.
- **The preview is informative without changing the simulation.** Mouse, touch and keyboard rotation let players inspect the selected vehicle even when that vehicle is only queued.
- **Readiness, hit outcomes and death use words as well as color.** “Fuel: fire / explosion” and “Crew knocked out · Back in 4.7 s” explain more than a generic health bar. Preserve the detail even if its placement changes.
- **The visual hierarchy is stronger than a global contrast score suggests.** The selected loadout row measured **9.20:1** text/background contrast. Desktop Play was **179×46 px**. Vehicle rows were **33 px high**: less generous than a 44 px touch target, but not automatically a failure of [WCAG 2.5.8’s 24 px minimum and exceptions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html). Fix the obstructed actions before changing every row.

## What the research means here

Laws of UX supplies **heuristics**, not proof of how these players feel. Hick’s Law argues for reducing the decision burden at a particular moment, not deleting six of the eight vehicles. Proximity and similarity explain why separating units from numbers or using one contact shape for opposing meanings raises the cost of reading. Fitts’s Law applies to selecting menu targets, **not** to making combat targets easier to hit. The [Doherty Threshold](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/) supports immediate feedback when hosting or changing loadout; its 400 ms productivity example is **not** an acceptable flight-input latency budget. The peak-end rule motivates testing disconnect and rematch flows, not assuming a measured effect on retention.

The Xbox guidelines cited above supply more specific design checks for **inputs, navigation, text and motion**. They are guidance for this browser game, not a claim of Xbox certification. The [Game Accessibility Guidelines basic checklist](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/) recommends adjustable sensitivity and interactive teaching. Celia Hodent’s [developer account of UX work at Epic](https://celiahodent.com/ux-practices-epic-games/) gives a useful example of attention and team-color ambiguity; it is practitioner experience, not user research on this game.

**Technical cross-check:** axe flagged `meta-viewport` in briefing, lobby and play; it could not settle contrast over the rendered scene. The project’s mechanical style scan reported no findings. Neither test evaluates the meaning of radar symbols or the legibility of moving WebGL content. The controls panel and radar together obscured substantially more of the field on portrait than desktop. At 640×360, the briefing had no horizontal overflow, but its scroll content was 535 px high in a 248 px panel. No claims about hearing accessibility or spoken captions are made; this review did not include listening tests.

| Technical area | Assessment (0–4) | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Accessibility | 2 | Labeled lobby fields, native buttons and visible resume focus; zoom restriction, tiny readouts and input/comfort gaps remain. |
| Performance | 3 | Offline ready in about 1.2 s here; the paused menu limits rendering to 15 FPS. The production JS alone is about 740 kB gzipped before assets. Test a constrained first visit before adding another preload or raising the menu frame cap. |
| Visual consistency | 3 | Selection, action and damage states are recognizable. HUD/briefing tokens coexist with hard-coded radar and X-ray colors; validate the latter in high-contrast and color-vision tests. |
| Responsive interaction | 2 | The preview supports touch and the compact menu scrolls, but the portrait action is below the fold and battle panels collide. |
| Implementation consistency | 3 | Preview state is separate from the live vehicle. Two sources of control instructions have already drifted; share bindings and labels before adding settings. |

These are prioritization judgments, not measured user satisfaction or a conformance score.

## Flight change verified alongside the audit

Real mouse/key input drove the local game; the QA clock supplied a fixed simulation interval for comparisons. Values are magnitudes for healthy aircraft, normal FOV, no free-look. Keyboard figures are per second at full input. Mouse results are per 100 CSS pixels after yaw settles.

| Input | Little Bird, before → after | Apache, before → after |
|---|---:|---:|
| Keyboard pitch | 54.43° → 87.09°/s | 40.82° → 65.32°/s |
| Keyboard roll | 51.57° → 82.51°/s | 38.67° → 61.88°/s |
| Mouse pitch / yaw / R-roll | 12.03° → 19.25° | 12.03° → 19.25° |

The yaw speed cap and response coefficient also increased 60%, rather than just letting the camera outrun the aircraft. Independent roll produced **0° of heading change** in the comparison. Tests cover both aircraft, full inversion, throttle isolation, and the host receiving guest pitch, roll and yaw. At full input, the Little Bird’s pitch flip is now about **4.1 s**, the Apache’s about **5.5 s**. These are tuning results, not evidence that every player prefers the faster default.

## Suggested next release and playtest

1. **First release:** fix the aircraft briefing, disclose the input requirement before deployment, enable browser zoom, and give the duel a shared ready state. These do not require a wholesale HUD redesign.
2. **Next:** build scalable HUD layouts and sensitivity/comfort controls. Then test the radar shapes, aircraft instruments, and queued-loadout confirmation together.
3. **Validate with players:** recruit newcomers and returning players, including keyboard-only and low-vision participants. Ask them to take off and recover, identify the nearest hostile with colors removed, queue a class away from base, and host/rematch a duel. Record first-attempt success, assistance needed and time to recover from an error. Compare against the same tasks on the current build; do not infer usability from frame rate or test passes alone.
